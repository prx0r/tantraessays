# VR0033_devotion_dec_3_2011.txt — full coverage (255 lines, ~7304 words)

88-minute lecture, 1 Dec 2011, on non-dual devotion (vr0033): the devotee-is-Shiva paradox, Shivoham, Utpaladeva's recognition doctrine, pure anupaya (no-method even for the perfected), Guru Tattva transmission analogies, knowledge=love, mantra-without-devotion useless, Kashi-as-holy-place, Tarak mantra close. Room interruptions and one 40x repetition block; otherwise one of the cleanest English files in the set.

## A — gold
- L1-3 (paradox frame + Upaladeva recognition).
  > "So this is 1st December, 2011, concerning devotion, what we call non-dual devotion. How Kashmiri Shaivite, who feels, who believes profoundly that his own consciousness, consciousness itself, is God"
  > "and that that is all that exists. How is he as a devotee of this God who is his own nature? So Upaladeva was a great exponent of the doctrine of recognition, which is the essence and the kind of extracted essence of the point of view of non-dualist Tantras."
- L5-7 (the hymn: Shankaracharya "I am He" + Shivoham refrain).
  > "And he sang this very loose translation of a hymn by Shankaracharya."
  > "Mind nor intellect nor ego feeling, Sky nor earth nor metals am I. I am He, I am He, Blessed Spirit ever free."
  > "Shivoham, Shivoham, Shivakevaloham, Chidanandarubha, Shivoham, Shivoham."
- L8-9 (gratitude/relief; mechanical morning vs one profound moment).
  > "So, what great sense of gratitude to be Lord Shiva. What a wonderful relief from the daily understanding of ourselves, with our problems, our desires, our frustrations."
  > "We sit in the morning, and we can repeat mechanically, but say sometime, a moment, when you can say that, with profound recognition, Shivoham, Shivoham."
- L10-11 (Shivo Bhutva Shiva Miyajit; non-dual devotion = ground of practice).
  > "So, this is how we worship Lord Shiva. Shivo Boutwa, Shiva Miyajit. He says, Being Lord Shiva, worship Lord Shiva."
  > "It is this which we call non-dual devotion. And that non-dual devotion, that insight, is the ground and foundation of all our practice."
- L14 (bhakti = to be/become a part of).
  > "Bhakti, Bhakti, Bhakti comes from the root bhakti, to mean to be a part of, to become a part of. So we as bhaktas, as devotees, we are parts of the Lord."
- L16 (God in every body → meat/pure-impure set aside).
  > "So it's this sense, you see, that God is present in every body, makes these traditions, they have their own social consequences. The sense that God is present as the reality of each and everything, through that by virtue of which everything exists, makes them set aside difference between meat and vegetarian, pure and impure."
- L19-21 (distraction test for the ethic).
  > "Now, of course, if you eat meat and it distracts you from that, you go, ah, lovely meat, or else, ah, I just had a lot of meat, horrible, shouldn't have done that, then better not."
  > "Then we don't, because you simply have to set that aside, because the most fundamental reality is that God Consciousness."
- L44-46 (oneness is simplest and hardest — the singing-teacher anecdote).
  > "One is as simple as can be, you know."
  > "So oneness, that pure simplicity of oneness, is the hardest. Otherwise we would all be one."
  > "So, it's like someone who hears singing, you know, and beautiful singing. Say, wow, I want to do that and start singing. Because they can't even sing a scale. After 40 years of music, I went to teacher and learned singing."
- L51-53 (silence → speech → universe; power = devotion through insight).
  > "We're not so stupid also, nor are we so clever. We know very well the power of silence."
  > "And that is called speech. It comes from the most profound silence of Supreme God consciousness."
  > "Now that power through which that all takes place, through which God expresses himself so wonderfully, that power we call devotion, means the devotion that comes through profound insight that God has of his own infinite being."
- L54 (servant-of-the-Lord: somehow, no method — pure anupaya character seed).
  > "Having somehow attained a state of a servant of the Lord. Somehow. Even He cannot understand that. Because He doesn't deserve it. He didn't do anything for it. There was no method. There was no way. He achieved it. Somehow."
- L60-63 (Abhinavagupta's concreteness: recognition-responses, not long "how nice it feels" verses; everything = Shiva's grace).
  > "But concretely, it results in what we do. Abhinavagupta, when he talks, he tells us how people have responded to their recognition,"
  > "For Abhinavagupta everything takes place only because of Shiva's grace. None of us can ever do anything, can ever be anything, so great, work so hard that we can merit that."
- L69 (the IS thesis statement — recognition as cause of all value).
  > "He says, I now explain with reason argument the doctrine of recognition, which is the cause of the accomplishment of every form of wealth, of all that is of value."
- L77-78 + L84 (sanctified-by-grace verse gloss; Anuppaya = nothing to do; Supreme State = recognition).
  > "Those who are sanctified in this way, means by Lord Shiva's grace, by the supremely real consciousness, firmly affirmed within them,"
  > "As He is holy, because He has made them such. This we call Anuppaya. Now means nothing to do."
  > "We begin from Supreme State. The Supreme State is a matter of recognition."
- L80 (the no-method rant — comedic plain-English gold).
  > "No means. Get up four in the morning, breathe as much as you like, stand on one leg, sell hundreds of Hare Krishna books, anything. Don't smoke, don't eat cooked food, no sex, heaven forbid. Anything you like, you think you should be done. But until this happens, we'll be struggling."
- L85 (Supreme Devotion = recognition; loves-as-He-loves-Himself).
  > "This is called Supreme Devotion, this recognition. Who can love God as He loves Himself? Not at all as we love ourselves, but as He loves all that is within Him."
- L89-90 (Bhairava-fire verse gloss + salt-mine simile).
  > "Before them manifests, in all respects, this totality of phenomenal existence, this whole world, all things, consumed in the fire of Bairada's consciousness."
  > "He quotes one nice thing, Jairata, says, just as wood, leaves or stones that have fallen into a salt mine become salt, in the same way existing things fallen into consciousness become salt."
- L92-94 (no mantra/no meditation for the sanctified).
  > "All that remains of the thoughts of pleasure and pain, doubt and fear, is supreme absorption in the pure light of consciousness, free of thought."
  > "For them there is no mantra, no mantra."
- L95-101 (sword of discernment; only task left = attend to the world; pure anupaya).
  > "Armed with the sword of discernment, he cuts through the net of all restrictions, the remaining observance for such a one, supremely sanctified and holy."
  > "has clearly only this left to do, namely, attend to the well-being of the world. This is pure anupair. He has nothing to do."
- L111-116 (Guru Tattva mechanism: master enters no-means, disciple observes, fragrance enters, elevated at once).
  > "The master enters his own no means consciousness and tells the disciple to observe what he is experiencing inwardly."
  > "As soon as he feels the fragrance of no-means consciousness, it enters into him at once, and he is elevated to it. He has nothing to do except sit before his master, and the disciple consciousness will get absolute entry into no-means consciousness."
- L121-122 (snake-poison + two-candles analogies).
  > "A cobra's poison enters somebody's body, the analogy of the snake's poison, and his individuality instantly ceases."
  > "When one candle is lit by another, it is not possible to say this candle is inferior and that one superior. Similarly, when a master transmits his power to his disciple, it is not possible to say that this was the master and that was the disciple. The two candles shine in the same way. This is Anupai. Nothing to do."
- L126 (knowledge = devotion identity — the essay's thesis line).
  > "Supreme Shakti Pant. So here what difference can there be between knowledge and devotion? To know Lord Shiva is to love Lord Shiva. To love Lord Shiva is to know Him."
- L134-136 (no criticism of dualist devotion; we begin separate too).
  > "No, no. We don't criticize anybody. We also begin by thinking Lord Shiva is separate."
- L148 (Parabhakti = realization; creating thoughts, beyond thoughts).
  > "Who is beyond thoughts. Because He is the one who is creating these thoughts in our minds. This we call devotion. This we call Parabhakti. Supreme devotion. That which you might call realization."
- L150 (knowledge/devotion inseparable via the knowing-love example).
  > "We cannot distinguish knowledge from devotion. How much more will I love this person, serve this person, care for this person, than I know him, than I know her."
- L165-166 (even the perfected continue meditation/scripture — Tantraloka as their scripture).

  > "We hear, we listen to the scripture. For us, this Tantraloka is our scripture."
- L169 (Shabda = Word, Christian parallel; even for Him).

- L171 (even in Anupaya He continues — maintain own consciousness + enlighten others).
  > "Even if you are in Anuppai, even if you receive, attain the Supreme State, He is saying, even He continues to do that. To maintain His own consciousness, even though that is not necessary, and also to enlighten others."
- L174-178 (praising the pure-anupaya master: devotion without meditation/mantra/rules; like Devodata).
  > "He is praising this Master who has attained this Anupaya, pure Anupaya, and who has done that, that has been achieved purely by Shiva's Lord, Shiva's grace."
  > "Salute, that one who is full of devotion, Bhakti Shali, he is Dhyani, means he knows Lord Shiva, that's why he is Bhakti Shali, he possesses devotion, and who is, not Dhyayatu, not Japataha, who without meditation, or without repeating mantra, and who without having to follow any rules,"
- L182-184 (wine-of-devotion verse: grey with worldly dust, young by devotion).
  > "I am young. By drinking the wine, my soul is young. By drinking the wine of your devotion, although I have become dusty, I am covered with the dust of many grey hairs that has come from,"
  > "I feel young, I feel young, I am young, he says by drinking the wine of your devotion."

  > "So this kind of certainty, we may sit, play sitar, we may write books, we may do anything, grow vegetables, whatever we are doing. This knowledge we call devotion. And this devotion we call knowledge."
