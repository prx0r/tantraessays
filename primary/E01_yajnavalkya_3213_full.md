# Brihadaranyaka 3.2.13 — Artabhaga question + secret answer (Max Muller SBE, public domain)

Sanskrit (Olivelle/UT Austin composite; ITRANS pending — see primary/README).
English (Max Muller):

> "Yagnavalkya," he said, "when the speech of this dead person enters into the fire, breath into the air, the eye into the sun, the mind into the moon, the hearing into space, into the earth the body, into the ether the self, into the shrubs the hairs of the body, into the trees the hairs of the head, when the blood and the seed are deposited in the water, where is then that person?"
>
> Yagnavalkya said: "Take my hand, my friend. We two alone shall know of this; let this question of ours not be discussed in public." Then these two went out and argued, and what they said was karman (work), what they praised was karman, viz. that a man becomes good by good work, and bad by bad work. After that Garatkarava Artabhaga held his peace.

PLAIN (key-derived, no jargon):
Where does the person go when each part returns to its element? Answer given aside, not on stage: what you do makes what you become. Good doing → good becoming. Bad doing → bad becoming.

Use: Essay 1 Beat 3. Mark gloss (karma-03) is reading of this exact passage.
